\section{Neural Time-Series Models in Traffic Analytics}

Recent work on traffic forecasting uses decomposition linear models~\cite{zeng2023transformers}, patch-based Transformers~\cite{nie2023patchtst}, and graph spatio-temporal networks~\cite{yu2018stgcn}. Deployed smart-city systems typically combine stream ingestion, model registries, and latency-aware inference rather than offline notebooks alone~\cite{its_bigdata2018,event_driven2025}. Flowmatic follows this pattern: classical preparation provides tabular baselines, while neural checkpoints handle modality-specific forecasting, reconstruction, and classification tasks selected by explicit routing rules.

\section{Scope of This Thesis}

The dissertation reports two linked parts that were actually completed:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a classical automated preparation study with ensemble anomaly detection and tree-based classification on Astana traffic data;
  \item a production implementation (\textbf{Flowmatic}) with neural extensions, adaptive routing, and reproducible platform experiments.
\end{enumerate}
Chapter~\ref{chap:results} presents both parts side by side and states where the platform extends or differs from the original manuscript design.
