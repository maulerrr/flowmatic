\section{Overview}
\label{sec:exp_overview}

This chapter answers the research questions in Section~\ref{sec:research_questions} through four evaluation layers:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Preparation and quality (RQ1)} --- measured outcomes on batch upload (DQI, cleaning, latency).
  \item \textbf{Platform validation (RQ2)} --- reproducible API experiments and the live Astana smart-city workbench.
  \item \textbf{Neural portfolio (RQ3)} --- eight Hugging Face checkpoints on held-out temporal test splits with three seeds ($s\in\{42,7,2026\}$), ablations, transfer, and streaming latency.
  \item \textbf{Routing (RQ4)} --- Manual/Auto registry behaviour in the workbench (quantitative router ablations deferred).
\end{enumerate}

Neural results are reproduced with \texttt{python models/paper/run\_phase3\_core.py}. Checkpoints are listed in Table~\ref{tab:production-portfolio}.

\subsection{Hold-out validation}

Neural models never observe the test partition during training. Windows follow temporal order: 70\% train, 15\% validation, 15\% test (Table~\ref{tab:split_protocol}). RMSE, masked MSE, and macro-F1 are computed on the test split only.

\subsection{Multi-seed robustness}

Each architecture is trained for seeds 42, 7, and 2026 with shared hyper-parameters (\texttt{phase3\_multiseed\_suite.yaml}). We report $\bar{m} \pm 1.96\, s/\sqrt{n}$ ($n=3$).

\section{Preparation and Quality Outcomes}
\label{sec:preparation_results}

Table~\ref{tab:upload-pipeline-experiment} reports an end-to-end batch run on \texttt{astana\_synthetic\_data.csv}: 30\,000 records ingested and cleaned in 406\,ms, quality score 100/100, and schema profiling (five numeric and four categorical columns). No blocking anomalies were flagged on this upload. These outcomes answer \textbf{RQ1} and \textbf{RQ2} for the batch path: preparation is automated, scored, and fast enough for interactive analyst use. Figure~\ref{fig:batch-upload-pipeline} matches the measured Docker Compose deployment.

\section{Neural Production Portfolio}
\label{sec:neural_portfolio}

\subsection{Portfolio}

Eight checkpoints in Table~\ref{tab:production-portfolio} cover forecasting (PatchTST, iTransformer, TimesBlock, STGCN, DLinear), anomaly reconstruction (TranAD), imputation (SAITS), and severity classification (Transformer). Formulations appear in Section~\ref{sec:neural_formulations}.

\subsection{Multi-seed test performance}

Table~\ref{tab:multiseed_aggregate} aggregates held-out test metrics:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Severity classification (Transformer):} macro-F1 = $0.911 \pm 0.017$.
  \item \textbf{Anomaly reconstruction (TranAD):} test RMSE = $0.035 \pm 0.004$ on normalised Astana windows.
  \item \textbf{Weather forecasting (TimesBlock):} RMSE = $0.034 \pm 0.002$ on HF weather.
  \item \textbf{Graph traffic (STGCN):} RMSE = $0.479 \pm 0.002$ on HF traffic subgraphs.
  \item \textbf{Traffic density (PatchTST):} RMSE = $0.561 \pm 0.004$ on Astana.
  \item \textbf{Imputation (SAITS):} masked MSE = $0.640 \pm 0.009$.
  \item \textbf{Energy (DLinear):} RMSE = $0.153 \pm 0.011$ on HF ETT.
  \item \textbf{Speed (iTransformer):} RMSE $\approx 0.999 \pm 0.001$ --- under the current training budget this slot does not meet deployment quality; it remains registered for routing experiments only.
\end{itemize}

Figure~\ref{fig:neural-multiseed} shows seed dispersion; Figure~\ref{fig:neural-forecast-rmse} ranks forecasting RMSE. Together these provide multi-task evidence for \textbf{RQ3}.

\section{Ablations and Generalisation}
\label{sec:ablations}

\subsection{Sequence length}

Table~\ref{tab:seq_ablation} and Figure~\ref{fig:seq-ablation} compare $L\in\{24,96\}$. Longer context helps DLinear on ETT (0.147 vs.\ 0.241 RMSE) but hurts PatchTST on Astana (0.807 vs.\ 0.559). Production default is $L=48$.

\subsection{Cross-dataset transfer}

Table~\ref{tab:cross_transfer} and Figure~\ref{fig:cross-transfer} train on one HF bundle and test on another without fine-tuning. RMSE degrades versus in-domain runs, indicating domain shift between energy and weather telemetry.

\subsection{Streaming inference latency}

Figures~\ref{fig:streaming-latency} and~\ref{fig:streaming-throughput} benchmark TorchScript exports. Mean single-event latency stays below 2\,ms for DLinear and TimesBlock and below 2\,s for large Transformers, informing smart-city deployment budgets.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Reproducibility.} Platform upload metrics and all neural aggregates in Table~\ref{tab:multiseed_aggregate} trace to repository scripts and published HF weights.

\textbf{External validity.} Astana remains semi-synthetic; METR-LA/PEMS hold-outs are planned. HF cross-transfer is a first generalisation probe.

\textbf{Anomaly operationalisation.} TranAD reports reconstruction RMSE on normalised windows; event-level AUROC against injected anomalies is future work.

\textbf{Explainability.} Insight Engine metrics and optional LLM summaries complement reconstruction residuals and imputation masks; LLM fidelity is not formally evaluated.

\textbf{Routing.} Manual/Auto routing is \textbf{implemented} in the workbench (\textbf{RQ4}); match-rate and oracle comparisons are future work.

\section{Summary}

Four conclusions follow: (1)~batch preparation achieves full quality scoring with sub-second processing on 30\,000 records; (2)~the Dockerised platform and Astana demo provide reproducible operational evidence; (3)~seven of eight neural slots show usable held-out test performance under a shared protocol, with iTransformer flagged as weak; (4)~registry routing \textbf{implements} modality-aware inference, pending quantitative evaluation.
