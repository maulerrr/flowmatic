\section{Background and Motivation}

Urban transportation management systems (UTMS) depend on timely, reliable sensor and event data. In practice, agencies receive CSV exports, API feeds, and streaming telemetry with missing values, inconsistent schemas, duplicate records, and domain-specific anomalies. Manual preparation is slow, difficult to audit, and hard to reproduce when models or analysts change~\cite{wang1996beyond,pipino2002data,sculley2015hidden}.

Research on automated preprocessing~\cite{liu2022automated,feurer2019auto,feurer2015autosklearn,mahasivam2017dataprep}, intelligent transportation systems~\cite{its_bigdata2018,zanella2014iot}, and neural traffic analytics~\cite{nie2023patchtst,yu2018stgcn,zeng2023transformers} often treats quality assessment, model training, and deployment as separate publications. Operational teams still lack a unified workflow that records quality diagnostics, applies consistent transformations, and serves neural specialists through a single registry with published artifacts~\cite{zaharia2018accelerating,kreutzberger2022mlops}.

This thesis designs, implements, and evaluates \textbf{Flowmatic}: a platform that automates quality assessment and cleaning, routes prepared streams to modality-specific neural checkpoints, and publishes reproducible training outputs on Hugging Face. The work extends the author's prior publications on adaptive passenger-flow modelling, automated data preparation, and event-driven traffic microservices~\cite{bakytuly2024hybrid,yedilkhan2025flowmatic,bakytuly2025adaptive,bakytuly2026microservices}.

\section{Scientific Novelty}
\label{sec:novelty}

The scientific novelty of this thesis is not a new neural architecture. It lies in the following elements, taken together:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Integrated quality--inference lifecycle.} A six-dimensional Data Quality Index is coupled to automated cleaning, batch export, and downstream neural inference under one auditable protocol, whereas prior works typically treat preparation, training, and deployment separately~\cite{wang1996beyond,liu2022automated,zaharia2018accelerating}.
  \item \textbf{Evidence-linked preparation under controlled defects.} Measurable DQI recovery is reported for injected corruptions on urban traffic uploads, not only for pristine exports~\cite{yedilkhan2025flowmatic}.
  \item \textbf{Unified multi-task evaluation.} Seven primary checkpoints plus one auxiliary iTransformer speed slot are compared under a single temporal hold-out design with reference baselines on identical or commensurate splits~\cite{nie2023patchtst,yu2018stgcn}.
  \item \textbf{Quantified registry routing.} Rule-based Auto routing is evaluated on a labelled simulator corpus with scenario-aligned oracles (density forecast without geolocation, graph forecast with geolocation, anomaly preference, severity classification)~\cite{bakytuly2026microservices}.
  \item \textbf{Reproducible artifact publication.} Training outputs, scaler statistics, and evaluation tables are published for independent verification~\cite{kreutzberger2022mlops,wolf2020transformers}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Problem Statement}

The central problem is how to automate data preparation for urban traffic data while preserving auditability and connecting prepared data to downstream models in both batch and streaming modes. Three sub-problems structure the investigation:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Preparation credibility.} Can measurable quality improve when uploads are corrupted, not only when files are already clean?
  \item \textbf{Inference credibility.} Do deployed neural models outperform simple baselines under a shared temporal protocol, and are metrics reported per task rather than as a single incomparable leaderboard?
  \item \textbf{Routing credibility.} Does Auto mode select checkpoint families that match labelled simulator events, and where does geo-priority policy diverge from oracle Astana forecast slots?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Research Objectives}

The thesis pursues five objectives aligned with completed work:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Implement and measure an automated preparation workflow with six-dimensional quality assessment, schema validation, and cleaning on urban traffic uploads.
  \item Deliver that workflow as a containerised web platform with authenticated upload, asynchronous processing, durable storage, and export to common destinations.
  \item Extend the platform with a smart-city pipeline that ingests simulated sensor streams, routes events through an adaptive core unit, and persists outputs in a medallion-style data lake~\cite{mazzetto2024urban}.
  \item Train and evaluate a portfolio of seven primary neural models (plus an auxiliary iTransformer speed slot) for forecasting, anomaly reconstruction, imputation, and severity classification on held-out temporal test splits with multi-seed replication.
  \item Validate the system through reproducible batch experiments, controlled corruption stress tests, baseline comparisons, and a quantitative routing pilot.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Research Questions}
\label{sec:research_questions}

\begin{description}
  \item[RQ1:] Does the Flowmatic preparation workflow improve measurable data quality on corrupted urban traffic uploads (DQI before/after, rows dropped, latency)?
  \item[RQ2:] Can preparation be operationalised as a reproducible batch service with reported end-to-end latency and quality scores on the Astana case study?
  \item[RQ3:] How do deployed neural models perform against majority-class, logistic regression, random forest, naive, and seasonal baselines under a common temporal hold-out protocol?
  \item[RQ4:] To what extent does rule-based Auto routing select scenario-aligned checkpoints on a labelled simulator corpus covering forecast, anomaly, classification, imputation, and weather profiles?
\end{description}

\section{Scope, Validation Layers, and Limitations}

\textbf{Two validation layers must not be conflated.} (1)~\emph{Batch platform validation} is demonstrated on the Astana CSV export through the Nest ingestion API (latency, quality score, cleaning). (2)~\emph{Neural training validation} uses Astana plus Hugging Face weather, ETT, and traffic-graph bundles prepared offline; those bundles are not all re-uploaded through the Nest API in this thesis. METR-LA and PEMS datasets are excluded from the evaluated dataset table (Table~\ref{tab:phase4_datasets}) because hold-out experiments were not completed for them in this study.

Experiments use semi-synthetic Astana data derived from an NDA-protected source (Appendix~\ref{app:astana}; generator rules in the unpublished companion study~\cite{bakytuly2026architectural}, continuing the published microservices work~\cite{bakytuly2026microservices}) with rule-based severity labels. The smart-city demo uses simulated feeds. \textbf{External municipal incident-label AUROC} and expert-labelled routing accuracy remain future work; injected-window TranAD AUROC is reported in Chapter~\ref{chap:results}. Live agency deployment and probabilistic forecast scores (CRPS) remain future work.
