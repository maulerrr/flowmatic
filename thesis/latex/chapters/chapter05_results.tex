\section{Overview}
\label{sec:exp_overview}

This chapter answers the research questions in Section~\ref{sec:research_questions} through three evaluation layers:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Classical preparation study} --- multi-dimensional DQI, tabular feature engineering, ensemble anomaly detectors, and XGBoost/Random Forest classification on Astana and public traffic benchmarks.
  \item \textbf{Platform validation} --- reproducible API experiments on the deployed Flowmatic stack (batch upload and live smart-city workbench).
  \item \textbf{Neural portfolio evaluation} --- eight modality-specific checkpoints on held-out temporal test splits with multi-seed replication ($s\in\{42,7,2026\}$), sequence ablations, cross-dataset transfer, and streaming inference latency.
\end{enumerate}

Neural results can be reproduced with \texttt{python models/paper/run\_phase3\_core.py}. Checkpoints are published on Hugging Face under \texttt{@pushthetempo/flowmatic-*}.

\subsection{Hold-out validation}

Neural models never observe the test partition during training. Windows are extracted in temporal order: earliest 70\% train, next 15\% validate, final 15\% test (Table~\ref{tab:split_protocol}). Reported RMSE, masked MSE, and macro-F1 are computed only on the test split.

\subsection{Multi-seed robustness}

Each architecture is trained independently for seeds 42, 7, and 2026 with fixed hyper-parameters (\texttt{phase3\_multiseed\_suite.yaml}). We report mean, standard deviation, and 95\% confidence interval $\bar{m} \pm 1.96\, s/\sqrt{n}$ across seeds ($n=3$).

\section{Classical Preparation Results}
\label{sec:classical_results}

The original dissertation compared automated preparation against raw data, simple preprocessing, and generic FeatureTools-style automation using XGBoost as the downstream classifier. On Astana, the full preparation pipeline achieved \textbf{0.9683 accuracy} and \textbf{0.9676 F1-score}, outperforming raw data (0.7823 accuracy) and generic AutoML features (0.9234). The ensemble anomaly detector (Isolation Forest + LOF + One-Class SVM) reached F1 = 0.858.

These results answer \textbf{RQ1}: domain-aware preparation improves tabular traffic classification. They remain valid as preparation-strategy baselines. Flowmatic extends this evidence by operationalising preparation as a service and adding neural task specialists for forecasting, imputation, and reconstruction-based anomaly scoring.

\section{Ablations and Generalisation}
\label{sec:ablations}

\subsection{Sequence length}

Table~\ref{tab:seq_ablation} and Figure~\ref{fig:seq-ablation} compare window lengths $L\in\{24,96\}$. Longer context helps DLinear on ETT (0.147 vs 0.241 RMSE) but hurts PatchTST on Astana density (0.807 vs 0.559). Production models use $L=48$ as a balanced default.

\subsection{Cross-dataset transfer}

Table~\ref{tab:cross_transfer} and Figure~\ref{fig:cross-transfer} report training on one HF bundle and testing on another without fine-tuning. Transfer RMSE remains finite but degrades relative to in-domain runs, indicating domain shift between energy and weather telemetry.

\subsection{Streaming inference latency}

Figures~\ref{fig:streaming-latency} and~\ref{fig:streaming-throughput} benchmark TorchScript exports on GPU with batch sizes $\{1,16,64\}$. Mean single-event latency stays below 2\,ms for compact models (DLinear, TimesBlock) and below 2\,s for large Transformers.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Test-set discipline.} Neural metrics in Table~\ref{tab:multiseed_aggregate} come from the held-out 15\% temporal test split. Platform upload metrics validate engineering latency and are complementary.

\textbf{External validity.} METR-LA and PEMS-BAY are ingested but not yet used as hold-out targets for Astana-trained models. Cross-dataset transfer within HF bundles provides a first generalisation check.

\textbf{Explainability.} Classical SHAP attributions on tree models are replaced in production by LLM-generated pipeline summaries and Insight Engine narratives. Neural models expose reconstruction residuals (TranAD) and imputation masks (SAITS) as diagnostics.

\textbf{Routing evidence.} Manual/Auto routing is implemented and demonstrated in the workbench (\textbf{RQ4}). Formal router-vs-oracle ablations, CRPS scores, and AUROC for anomaly events are not yet reported and are listed as future work.

\section{Summary}

The experimental record supports four conclusions aligned with the research questions: (1)~automated classical preparation improves tabular traffic classification; (2)~Flowmatic implements this workflow as a reproducible service with measured batch latency; (3)~the eight-model neural portfolio provides multi-dataset, multi-seed evidence on held-out test data with published artifacts; (4)~registry-based routing enables modality-aware inference in the smart-city pipeline, with deeper routing analysis left for future work.
